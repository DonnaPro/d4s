# Content ideas 13 to 22: revised, wider lens

**Revised:** 16 September 2026
**Supersedes:** `15-content-ideas-13-to-22-review.md`
**Why revised:** the first pass scored these almost entirely on "can this rank in Google". That is the wrong primary test for a programme whose stated objective is AI citation, and it ignored internal linking, entity reinforcement and sales value. Zoran was right to push back.

---

## What changed in the method

Six ways a page can earn its place, not one:

| Value | Test |
|---|---|
| **Organic** | Is there demand, and can we win it |
| **AI citation** | Is it the shape an assistant quotes, on a prompt where competitors are named and we are not |
| **Internal link** | Does it feed a page that is stranded or under-powered |
| **Entity** | Does it reinforce a concept the site needs to own |
| **Sales** | Does it answer something that loses deals |
| **Risk** | Cannibalisation, and whether it is manageable rather than binary |

A page needs to score on **two or more**. Ranking alone is not required, and was never the point.

## 14. Private equity: why this flips

**My first answer was wrong, but so is the reason Zoran offered for reversing it.**

It is not that the article would pass links to `/who-we-serve/investment-virtual-assistant/`. That page has 52 inbound links already and ranks for nothing. More links will not fix it.

It flips for a different reason: **service pages do not get cited, and guides do.**

Look at what the AI citation data actually shows. The most-cited page on the site is `/guides/executive-assistant-to-ceo/` at 24 mentions. Not one `/who-we-serve/` page appears anywhere in the citation data, and nor does any service page. The August research told the same story: the cited pages were comparisons and guides.

So `/who-we-serve/investment-virtual-assistant/` existing does **not** mean the private-equity opportunity is covered. That page can never do the job an editorial guide does, because its format is wrong for the surface that matters. This is the same distinction that made `/virtual-pa/` worth building even though `/virtual-executive-assistant/` existed.

Add to that:
- `executive assistant private equity` at 50/mo UK is the third-highest term in the batch
- Financial services is 15 per cent of the client base, so there is direct commercial value independent of traffic
- Anonymised case studies are now cleared, and PE is exactly where a concrete example lands hardest

**Cannibalisation is manageable and must be managed explicitly.** The guide covers what to delegate, compliance and cost. The `/who-we-serve/` page is the offer. Different intent, different format, and the guide links down to the service page rather than competing with it. No shared head term: the guide targets `executive assistant private equity`, the service page keeps its own.

**Link job:** feed `/guides/how-to-build-sops-to-delegate/` and `/ceo-insights/white-collar-repricing/`, both orphans and both topically adjacent.

## 15. Tech startups: still no

The same argument could be made, and it is weaker on every input.

`executive assistant for startups` carries 20 searches a month against private equity's 50. More importantly, the editorial slot for this audience is already filled: `/comparison/best-virtual-assistant-companies-startup-founders/` is a guide, not a service page, and it was widened with early, growth and established sections three weeks ago. The gap that justifies #14 does not exist here, because the guide-format coverage is already in place.

**Unless** you want the compliance and cost angle specifically, in which case it belongs as two sections inside the existing comparison page rather than as a new URL.

## 17. Europe: same verdict, better reason

Not "no value". The value is real and the page already exists.

`/guides/how-to-hire-virtual-assistant-europe/` is **an orphan with zero inbound contextual links and zero rankings**. It is not failing because Europe is a bad idea; it is failing because nothing links to it and nobody has worked on it.

Olivia's proposal, a country-by-country breakdown linking to the 20 country pages, is exactly the right shape. It should be built into that URL. That gets the hub, fixes an orphan, and avoids putting two of your own pages on the same territory.

**And the country pages are where the actual demand is.** `virtual assistant ireland` is 720/mo with `/countries/ireland/` already at position 8. The pan-European terms are all at the reporting floor in every market including Germany and the Netherlands. Europe is a linking structure, not a search term.

## 22. Board and investor delegation: write it, the blocker is separate

Revised from "blocked". `/services/managing-investors/` being `noindex` stops that page benefiting, but it does not stop the article being worth writing:

- It answers a prompt shape competitors get named on, at the moment of peak admin load
- Both `/virtual-executive-assistant/` and `/virtual-pa/` carry an investor and stakeholder card that nothing on the site explains in practice
- Anonymised fundraising examples are strong material
- It can feed two orphans regardless of the service page's status

**Still flag the noindex separately.** Eight `/services/*` pages are live and absent from the sitemap, including 2,800 words on managing investors. That decision is worth more than this article, and it is independent of it.

**Link job:** `/ceo-insights/ceo-time-management-strategy/` and `/guides/executive-assistant-to-ceo/`, the latter being the most-cited page and worth strengthening.

## 20. Tech stack: moves up, but stays last

Given a job, it earns a place. `/guides/how-to-build-sops-to-delegate/` and `/guides/ai-for-founders/` are both delegation-cluster pages, the first an orphan, and a tooling article is the natural parent for both.

It stays last because tool lists decay faster than anything else you can publish and it is the only idea here that creates permanent maintenance work. Write it when the queue is empty, not before.

## Two things worth more than items 6 to 9

**`/countries/ireland/` sits at position 8 on a 720/mo term** with no dedicated work. That is larger than any term attached to any article in this batch. Germany and the Netherlands show the same pattern smaller.

**Eighteen pages have zero contextual inbound links**, including six `/ceo-insights/` articles that are finished editorial nobody can reach. Linking those from existing pages costs a morning and needs no new writing at all.

Both of those are cheaper than an article and more certain in return. They should sit above most of this list, not below it.

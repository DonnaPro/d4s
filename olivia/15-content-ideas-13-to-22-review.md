## 13. How ADHD Founders Can Make Delegation Actually Work

**Verdict: yes, with guardrails.**

**If yes:** `adhd entrepreneur` carries 140 searches a month and `adhd founder` another 20, which is more real demand than most ideas in this batch. Nothing on the site touches it, so there is no cannibalisation risk at all. Most importantly, Olivia has first-hand material: she worked with an ADHD founder as a freelance EA, and Saga has a current client. That is genuine experience, which is the one input competitors cannot copy and the thing Google's guidance actually rewards. The delegation angle is also specific rather than generic: the reason delegation fails for ADHD founders is different from why it fails for everyone else, and that specificity is what makes a page quotable.

**If no:** it is adjacent to a clinical topic without being one, and that carries a handling risk. Anything that reads as advice about managing a condition rather than about running a business invites a quality problem the site does not currently have. It is also a narrower audience than the headline volume suggests, since most of those 140 searches are not founders looking to hire.

**What decides it:** write it as a delegation article for a specific kind of operator, not as anything resembling health content. No claims about ADHD itself, no symptoms, no advice about managing the condition. Anonymised client examples are the whole value, so if those cannot be cleared for use, the piece loses its reason to exist and becomes a generic delegation article, which the site already has three of.

## 14. Executive Assistant for Private Equity

**Verdict: no.**

**If yes:** `executive assistant private equity` carries 50 searches a month, which is more than most terms in this batch, and financial services is 15 per cent of the client base, so the commercial logic is real. Anonymised EA case studies would give it something the existing pages lack.

**If no:** `/who-we-serve/investment-virtual-assistant/`, `/who-we-serve/financial-advisor-virtual-assistant/` and `/who-we-serve/fintech-virtual-assistant/` already exist and all rank for zero. The four `/guides/executive-assistant-for-*/` pages this would copy also rank for zero. This is the third run at a strategy that has produced nothing across 25 pages. The reason those pages fail is not that private equity was missing from the list, it is that each page is too thin to be retrieved, and a twenty-sixth will be equally thin.

**What decides it:** if you want the financial-services vertical to work, the answer is to make **one** of the existing pages genuinely strong rather than add another weak one. Take `/who-we-serve/investment-virtual-assistant/`, put the anonymised case studies in it, give it the compliance and cost detail Olivia is proposing, and see whether depth fixes what breadth did not. That is the same experiment at a fraction of the cost, and it tests the actual hypothesis.

## 16. 15 Ways Real Founders Use Their Executive Assistant

**Verdict: yes, conditional.**

**If yes:** the format is well suited to AI retrieval. An enumerated list of specific, concrete uses is exactly the shape an assistant extracts from when someone asks what an EA actually does. It also uses the one asset that cannot be replicated: real anonymised examples from EAs across multiple industries. Competitors write this article from imagination; DonnaPro can write it from client work. And it supports `/guides/executive-assistant-to-ceo/`, which is currently the most AI-cited page on the site.

**If no:** there is no search demand for it, so it lives or dies on citation alone. There is also a real overlap risk with the task lists already on `/virtual-executive-assistant/`, `/virtual-pa/` and `/guides/inbox-calendar-travel-management-executive-assistant/`. If it becomes another list of EA tasks, it competes with three existing pages and adds nothing.

**What decides it:** the word "real" in the title. Fifteen genuine, specific, slightly surprising examples from actual client work is a strong page. Fifteen generic tasks is a fourth copy of something you already have. If the case studies cannot be gathered and cleared, do not write it.

## 17. How to Hire a Virtual Assistant in Europe

**Verdict: no. It already exists.**

**If yes:** Olivia cites 725 GSC impressions on `virtual assistant europe` at 6.34 per cent CTR, and a country-by-country breakdown linking to the 20 country pages would be a sensible hub.

**If no:** **`/guides/how-to-hire-virtual-assistant-europe/` is already live.** So is `/comparison/top-5-virtual-assistant-services-europe/`, and so are 20 `/countries/*` pages. The existing Europe guide ranks for **zero** UK keywords. On demand, `virtual assistant europe` returns **10 searches a month**, not 725: impressions measure how often a page appeared, usually far down the results, not how many people search. Writing a second Europe guide would put two of your own pages against each other for a term neither currently wins.

**What decides it:** the idea is not wrong, the page is. Rework `/guides/how-to-hire-virtual-assistant-europe/` into the hub Olivia describes, with the country-by-country breakdown and internal links. Same work, no new URL, no cannibalisation, and it fixes a page that is currently earning nothing.

## 18. What 5 Things You Cannot Delegate to a Virtual Assistant

**Verdict: yes.**

**If yes:** negative-space content is disproportionately quotable. When someone asks an assistant what a VA cannot do, there is very little honest content to draw on, because vendors do not write about their own limits. It also matches the positioning that already earns DonnaPro citations: the comparison pages get quoted precisely because they admit where DonnaPro is not the answer. It is cheap, it is short, and it carries almost no cannibalisation risk because no existing page covers the boundary.

**If no:** `what virtual assistants cannot do` has no measurable volume, so it is a pure citation play with no organic floor. There is also a sales risk in publishing a list of things you cannot do, and it needs writing carefully so the takeaway is confidence rather than limitation.

**What decides it:** keep the list genuinely short and genuinely honest. Olivia is right that the shortness is the point. Five real limits, each with what to do instead, and the reader finishes more convinced rather than less. If it turns into five soft non-limits, it reads as evasive and does the opposite.

## 19. How to Know If Your Assistant Is a Strategic Partner

**Verdict: yes. The strongest of the zero-volume ideas.**

**If yes:** it attacks the objection that actually loses deals. "I will hire in-house because a virtual EA cannot be strategic" is the belief standing between a founder and the purchase, and no page on the site addresses it directly. Olivia arrived at this from shadowing Saga, so it comes from observed reality rather than positioning. It is also a diagnostic framework, which is a shape assistants quote well: a reader can apply it to their own situation.

**If no:** it sits close to `/comparison/virtual-assistant-vs-executive-assistant/` and to the new decision guide at `/comparison/ea-vs-va-vs-chief-of-staff/`, both of which distinguish task execution from judgement. Without a clear boundary it becomes a third page making the same distinction. `strategic executive assistant` has 10 searches a month, so again there is no organic floor.

**What decides it:** the boundary. The existing pages answer "what is the difference between these roles". This one must answer "how do I tell which one I currently have", aimed at someone who **already employs an assistant** and suspects they are underusing them. That is a different reader and a different moment. Written that way it is additive. Written as another comparison it is a duplicate.

## 20. The Executive Assistant Tech Stack

**Verdict: defer.**

**If yes:** it connects naturally to the delegation guides, and tooling is a practical concern for someone about to onboard an assistant.

**If no:** `executive assistant tools` and `tools for executive assistants` carry 10 searches a month each; `executive assistant tech stack` has none. `/guides/ai-for-founders/` already covers adjacent ground and ranks for zero. Tool lists also decay faster than anything else you can publish: half the tools will have repriced or been acquired within a year, and a stale tool list actively damages credibility. It is the only idea here that creates permanent maintenance work.

**What decides it:** nothing about it is wrong, it is just the weakest use of the next writing slot. If you want the content, it belongs as a section inside `/guides/working-with-executive-assistant/`, which already targets the onboarding moment and currently ranks for nothing, rather than as a page that needs revisiting every year.

## 21. X Golden Concepts from The E-Myth Revisited

**Verdict: yes. Do this one first.**

**If yes:** this is the only idea in the batch with meaningful search demand and a proven template on the same site.

| Term | UK volume |
|---|---|
| `e-myth revisited` | **170** |
| `emyth` | 90 |
| `the e myth revisited summary` | 10 |

I checked whether a company blog can actually compete in a book SERP. For `buy back your time`, the results are Amazon, the author's own site, Audible, Waterstones, YouTube, a summary site, and **DonnaPro at position 10**. The SERP for `e-myth revisited` has the identical shape: Amazon, Goodreads, Reddit, emyth.com, Audible, Waterstones, YouTube, and readingraphics, a book-summary site that ranks on both. So the format ranks, the site has already proven it can rank with it, and the target book has a comparable SERP.

`/ceo-insights/buy-back-your-time/` is one of only two pages on the whole site holding a top-10 position on a term with real volume. Replicating what works is a better bet than any of the untested formats in this batch.

**If no:** the audience match is looser than Dan Martell. The E-Myth is about systematising a small business, not about buying back a founder's time, so the bridge to executive assistants is longer and needs building carefully rather than assumed. There is also a limit to how many times this trick works before the site looks like a book-summary blog.

**What decides it:** the bridge. Buy Back Your Time hands you the delegation argument for free. The E-Myth hands you "work on the business, not in it", which is one step away. The piece has to make that step explicitly, most likely through the Technician-Manager-Entrepreneur framework: the founder stuck as Technician is the person who needs an assistant. Get that right and it works. Skip it and it is a book summary with a CTA bolted on.

## 22. Board Prep, Investor Updates and Fundraising Admin

**Verdict: yes, but something is broken first.**

**If yes:** it catches a founder at a moment of peak admin load, which is exactly when support gets bought. It supports `/services/managing-investors/`, a 2,800-word page Olivia correctly identifies as high-value and low-visibility. `/virtual-executive-assistant/` and `/virtual-pa/` both carry an investor and stakeholder card but nothing explains what that delegation looks like in practice. Fundraising founders are also a high-value segment.

**If no:** none of the obvious terms has measurable volume. `executive assistant board prep`, `investor relations assistant` and `board meeting preparation` are all at or near zero, so this is a pure citation and positioning play.

**What decides it, and this is the important part:** **`/services/managing-investors/` is set to `noindex`.** It returns 200, it has 2,800 words, and it is excluded from the sitemap. Whatever impressions Olivia found in GSC are historical or from before it was hidden. Writing editorial content to support a page Google is told to ignore achieves nothing.

So the sequence is: decide whether that page should be indexed. It is one of at least eight `/services/*` pages live but absent from the sitemap, which I flagged earlier. If they are meant to be indexed, that is a far bigger and cheaper win than any article in this batch: 2,800 words of existing service copy currently earning nothing. If they are deliberately hidden, say so and this article has no destination to support.

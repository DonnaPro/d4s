## 3. Cross-document consistency: the main systemic problem

The same statistics appear in several articles with different values. Individually most are defensible. Together they read as careless, and an AI assistant comparing two DonnaPro pages will find them disagreeing.

**The Asana "work about work" figure, four uses:**

| Document | Figure | Cited as | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision guide | 60% | Asana 2023 | `work-isnt-working` |
| Outsource admin | 60% | Asana 2023 | `work-isnt-working` |
| Startup founders | 60% | Asana **2021** | `anatomy-of-work-summary` |

I fetched these. The `work-isnt-working` page does support **60%**. The businesswire release carrying **58%** blocks automated fetching, so I could not confirm it, but both numbers are plausible because Asana publishes this index annually and the figure moves. **Pick one figure, one year, one URL, and use it everywhere.**

**The Harvard Business Review CEO time study, four different claims:**

| Document | Claim |

The HBR page is paywalled, so I could not verify any of these directly. Two points stand regardless. ## 4. Blocking: fix before publishing

**4.1 The DonnaPro claims in `when-to-hire` are unsourced and contradict each other.** This is the one item I would call serious.

> "According to DonnaPro's experience across hundreds of founder engagements, the typical CEO who hires their first EA is running a 5-20 person company and spending approximately **10 hours per week** on admin"

> "If it is above **15 hours** per week (the DonnaPro average at point of first engagement) you are already past the threshold"

> "The Growth-Stage Founder: spending **15-20 hours** per week on operational tasks"

> "All three profiles share the **10-hour-per-week** admin pattern at point of engagement"

The DonnaPro average is stated as 10 hours and as 15 hours in the same article, and the client profile that is meant to illustrate it says 15-20. Also unverified: "hundreds of founder engagements", the three client profiles, and "one to two weeks" to a fully operational EA.

The style guide tells her to stop and ask you about any claim about the service she is unsure of. She did not. **Either give her the real numbers or cut these sentences.** Invented operational claims are the most expensive kind of error on a page that already gets quoted — a reader or an AI assistant will repeat them back to you.

**4.2 A dead internal link.** The decision guide suggests `/comparison/outsourced-executive-assistant-cost-uk/`. It returns **404** and is not in the sitemap. She meant either `/comparison/outsourced-executive-assistant/` or `/guides/virtual-executive-assistant-cost-uk/`. Every one of her other 23 link targets resolves.

**4.3 A dead external source, on the protected page.** In `how-to-hire-virtual-assistant-uk`, the REC citation at `rec.uk.com/our-view/research/recruitment-and-the-economy` returns **404**. It carries two statistics: the 42-day average time to fill a vacancy and the £132,000 cost of a poor management hire. Both need a working source or both come out.

**4.4 "I can not justify" appears in two headings.** In `when-to-hire`: the H3 *"I can not justify spending £70k on an EA"* and the FAQ *"I think I need an EA but I can not justify spending £70k on one yet"*. Removing contractions turns "can't" into **"cannot"**, one word. "Can not" means something different and reads as a typo. She gets this right elsewhere in the same document ("a headcount decision you cannot reverse"), so it is a slip, but it is in headings, which is where it will be seen and quoted.

**4.5 A currency slip in the buy-back article.** The Buyback Rate worked example reads: *"Divide £140,000 by 2,000 to get **$70/hour**"*. Pounds in, dollars out. This is pre-existing article text rather than hers, but she was working in that document and it should not survive the edit.

**4.6 A single-company case study presented as a general finding.** Also in buy-back:

> "A Yale School of Management case study on SME leadership found that CEOs spend approximately 90% of their time on operations rather than strategy"

The paper is titled *"On the Nature of CEO Time Allocation in an SME"* — one company. Stated as "CEOs spend", it becomes a claim about all CEOs. Either attribute it as one case study explicitly, or replace it. The sources table also dates it 2021 while the URL path says 2025.

**4.7 A statistic sourced to a homepage.** Also in buy-back: *"97% of business owners work weekends, and 79% feel they are putting in too many hours (The Alternative Board - https://www.thealternativeboard.com)"*. That is the homepage, not the research. The style guide's rule is that if she cannot point to the page carrying the figure, the claim comes out.

**4.8 Prices still hardcoded where placeholders were used elsewhere.** In the cost guide she converted the body to `[DONNAPRO PRICE EUR]` but left live figures in three places: the answer box ("a €2,700/month EA delivers a 344% ROI"), the ROI summary ("€9,300 in monthly value"), and the new company-size table, whose band edges are €2,700 and €6,500 — both actual DonnaPro prices. Same fix as she already applied to the rest of the document. Her arithmetic is right, incidentally: 60 × €200 = €12,000, less €2,700 is €9,300, which is 344%.

## 5. Style-guide misses

These are mechanical and quick, but they are systematic rather than one-offs.

**Answer boxes and FAQ answers run long.** The rule is 60-90 words for an answer box and 40-90 for an FAQ answer, because that is the length an AI assistant quotes whole.

| Item | Words | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Decision guide answer box | **125** | 90 |
| Outsource admin answer box | **103** | 90 |
| Decision guide FAQ "what roles can take admin off a CEO's plate" | **130** | 90 |
| Decision guide FAQ "my weekly schedule is packed" | **151** | 90 |
| Decision guide FAQ "realistic budget" | 109 | 90 |
| Outsource admin FAQ "how do I go about outsourcing" | 104 | 90 |

Not one answer box or new FAQ answer in the two new articles is inside the limit. In fairness, part of this is my brief: I asked her to fit five roles and their costs into the decision guide's answer box and also to keep it to 90 words. Those two instructions fight each other. But the FAQ overruns have no such excuse, and the longest is nearly double.

**Images: light, and the metadata is sloppy.**

- Two images in a 3,572-word guide and two in a 2,935-word guide. The rule is one every 800-1,000 words, so three to four each.
- One filename was never renamed from the stock original: `portrait-corporate-woman-suit-working-office-using-laptop-typing-does-her-job-coworking.jpg`. The guide asks for short kebab-case descriptions like `ea-travel-planning-desk.jpg`.
- That same image's `FILE:` line does not match the actual Drive filename, which has `(1)` appended. You will not be able to match them up automatically.
- Alt text: `"A ceo working on his laptop..."` and `"A stressed ceo sitting at his desk with his head in his hands"`. Lowercase "ceo" should be "CEO", and both assume the subject is male where the image does not require it. One alt text describes clothing ("in a red top") rather than the scene, which is not what alt text is for.

**Two empty sections in the research pack.** Under Google People Also Ask, both searches end with *"Questions included:"* and then nothing. The actual PAA questions are missing for both "virtual PA" and "hire a virtual PA". She clearly had them — she refers to "the People Also Ask screenshots reviewed" and salvages four pricing variants in the next section — but the lists did not make it into the document. This was one of the five things the brief specifically asked for, so it needs adding.

There is also an empty *"Example:"* heading under Images with nothing beneath it.

**Images declined in the research pack.** The brief asked for "images, named and alt-texted as usual". She argued instead that service pages should be copy-led and suggested video testimonials and a collage of real assistants. The reasoning is sound and I would take the suggestion, but she should have said so as a question rather than substituting her own answer for the deliverable.

**Every source URL in the research pack carries `?utm_source=chatgpt.com`.** Eight of them. Harmless, but strip them. It also tells us she researched via an AI assistant, which is fine, but it makes the first-hand verification of those competitor prices worth a second look given how specific they are.

**Handover formatting.**

- `how-to-hire-virtual-assistant-uk` does not use the `[LINK → /path/]` convention at all. Six internal links are named in prose with no target given ("see our guide to C-level executive assistants", "our comparison of EA agency vs freelance"), which leaves you to work out where they point. Every one of those pages exists, so it is just an omission.
- Several tables have `**bold**` typed as literal characters inside the cells instead of real bold formatting. It survives conversion badly.
- `ConnXess` is listed in the outsource-admin Sources Reviewed table and in the methodology paragraph but is never actually cited in the body.

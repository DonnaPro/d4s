# Strategy review: the two new articles

**Reviewed:** 9 September 2026
**Documents:** `NEW_Do You Need an EA, a VA, a Chief of Staff, or Something Else?` and `NEW_How to Outsource Your Admin as a CEO`
**Question asked:** are they good, and do they fit the SEO and AI-visibility strategy?
**Method:** UK search volume and CPC from DataForSEO; live top-10 SERPs for six queries; domain-level overlap against the pages these could cannibalise; SERP result-type composition.

---

## Verdict

**Both fit the strategy. Publish both.** One is stronger than the brief assumed and one is weaker, and neither weakness is in the writing.

- **Outsource admin is the better asset.** The gap Olivia identified is real, measurable and commercially valuable, and it does not collide with anything you own. It is also the one where the brief's shape may be wrong.
- **The decision guide is a pure AI-citation play**, exactly as the brief said. Confirmed: zero search demand on every query it could target. Judge it on citations only.
- **The two articles overlap each other** more than either overlaps anything existing. That is the one thing to fix before publishing, and it is the failure mode the brief specifically warned about.

## 1. Outsource admin: the gap is real, and it is worth more than expected

Olivia's finding was that every title on the site uses "executive assistant" language and none uses "admin". The volume data says she found something.

| Keyword | UK volume | CPC |
|---|---|---|
| `virtual admin support` | 40 | - |
| `outsource admin` | 30 | **£46.95** |
| `outsourced administration` | 30 | £46.95 |
| `outsourced admin support` | 20 | **£241.52** |
| `outsource administrative tasks` | 10 | - |
| **`outsourced executive assistant`** (for comparison) | **10** | - |

Roughly 130 searches a month across the cluster, and a £241 CPC on `outsourced admin support`. That is one of the highest cost-per-click figures I have seen in this market, and CPC is the cleanest available proxy for commercial intent. For context, the head term of a protected AI-cited page, `outsourced executive assistant`, carries a third of the volume of `outsource admin`.

**DonnaPro appears nowhere in either SERP.** The competitive set is entirely different from the one you track: adminoutsourcingservices.co.uk, sedulousadmin.co.uk, fridaygirl.com, theadministrationhub.co.uk, cheshirevirtualpa.com, qxaccounting.com. This is uncontested ground for you.

### The cannibalisation question, now tested rather than asserted

The brief claimed this article "does not overlap `/comparison/outsourced-executive-assistant/` - different results, different intent". That was an assertion from titles, which is the reasoning that has been wrong before on this site. Tested:

| Query pair | Domain overlap | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `outsource admin` vs `outsourced executive assistant` | **0%** | Distinct. Keep separate |
| `outsourced admin support` vs `outsourced executive assistant` | **0%** | Distinct. Keep separate |

Not one shared domain in either comparison. The assertion was right, and it is now evidence. **No risk to the protected page.**

### But the brief may have specified the wrong page type

Look at what actually ranks. On `outsourced admin support`, **eight of the nine organic results are vendor service pages**, not articles: adminoutsourcingservices.co.uk (homepage), sedulousadmin.co.uk, fridaygirl.com/admin-department/, theadministrationhub.co.uk, qxaccounting.com/uk/service/virtual-admin-services/, finlink.co.uk/outsourced-ifa-admin/, rsvp.co.uk/services/administrative-support/, isp-group.org/en/administrative-outsourcing/. The single editorial result is a guide from a virtual PA firm.

On `outsource admin` the split is roughly four service pages, two editorial, and three others including a Companies House record and a Reddit thread.

**This is the same finding as the Virtual PA research**, where the brief correctly concluded that "every result on that search is a vendor offer page, so a long explainer would be the wrong shape". The same logic applies here and the brief did not apply it.

So the recommendation splits:

- **Publish the guide as written.** It is well matched to `how to outsource admin` and the surrounding long tail, it is the right shape for the AI surface (an AI Overview is present on both queries), and Reddit and a cost guide both rank, so editorial content does compete here.
- **Then consider a service page at `/outsource-admin/`**, built on the `/virtual-executive-assistant/` template, to go after the £241 CPC term. That is a separate decision and a separate piece of work, and it is the higher-value half. The guide can link up to it.

### One thing to watch

Result four on `outsource admin` is `uk.indeed.com/q-outsourcing-administrator-jobs.html`. There is no Google Jobs pack on either query, so the intent is predominantly commercial, but "admin" is a job-seeker word and one job board is already in the top five. Given the whole careers-versus-client problem this site has just spent a month fixing, the article must read unambiguously buyer-side throughout, and the slug must not drift anywhere near "jobs" or "administrator". It currently reads correctly.

## 2. Decision guide: confirmed as a citation-only asset

Every query this article could target returns no measurable UK volume:

| Keyword | UK volume |
|---|---|
| `ea or va` | none |
| `do i need an executive assistant` | none |
| `should i hire an executive assistant` | none |
| `what kind of assistant do i need` | none |

The brief said "nobody searches for this in Google. That is fine. Judge it purely on whether an AI assistant would quote it." That is now confirmed rather than assumed. **Do not measure this page on rankings or clicks.** Its only success metric is whether it starts getting cited on the four prompts it was built for.

Its construction is right for that purpose: five roles compared side by side, the cost objection answered directly, a delegation task list, a readiness checklist, and FAQs phrased as real first-person questions. That is the correct shape for retrieval.

### The overlap it does carry

The article's central section compares five roles, and two of those pairings already have their own pages: `/comparison/virtual-assistant-vs-executive-assistant/` and `/guides/chief-of-staff-vs-executive-assistant/`.

Since the decision guide has no organic target, the risk is not that it steals rankings. The risk is a third page competing for the same citations. It is mitigated, because the existing pages answer "what is the difference" while this one answers "which should I hire", and she links out to both rather than re-arguing them. Keep it that way: if the five-role table ever grows into a full pairwise argument, it becomes a competitor to two of your own pages.

### A baseline reading worth recording

While testing, two live results are worth writing down as the starting point for the six-to-eight week measurement window:

- On `virtual assistant vs executive assistant` (10/mo), the DonnaPro result at position 3 is **`/careers/resources/what-is-executive-virtual-assistant/`** - the careers page. `/comparison/virtual-assistant-vs-executive-assistant/`, the client page built for that query, is not in the top 10.
- On `chief of staff vs executive assistant` (20/mo), **no DonnaPro URL ranks at all**, despite `/guides/chief-of-staff-vs-executive-assistant/` existing.

Fix 1 went live on 8 September, one day before this check, so this is expected lag and not a failure. But it is the cleanest possible baseline: the careers page still holds a pure buyer-comparison query today. If it is still there in six weeks, the fixes did not work. Add both queries to the weekly tracking list.

For completeness, the two existing comparison queries overlap each other by 44%, and every shared domain is a generic publisher (Reddit, LinkedIn, YouTube, wishup.co). They are legitimately separate pages.

## 3. The one real problem: the two articles overlap each other

This is the failure mode the brief warned about in its own words: "splitting one intent across several new pages makes each of them thinner, and thin pages do not get cited."

Shared substance across the two drafts:

| Element | Decision guide | Outsource admin |
|---|---|---|
| VA / managed EA / bookkeeper cost comparison, same figures | "Can I justify this yet?" | "Step 3: Choose the right type of support" |
| Delegatable task list | "How to know what to delegate" | "Step 2: Decide what to outsource first" |
| Asana 60% statistic | Yes | Yes |
| HBR CEO time statistic | Yes | Yes |
| "Define outcomes, not steps" | Readiness checklist | "Step 4: Set it up" |
| "Start part-time" recommendation | Yes | Yes |

Both articles currently answer **which** kind of support to buy and **how** to set it up. The clean split is one each:

- **Decision guide owns WHICH.** Keep the five-role comparison, the cost objection, the readiness checklist. Cut "How to know what to delegate" down to a short paragraph and link across.
- **Outsource admin owns HOW.** Keep the audit, the setup, the communication rhythm, the 90-day measurement. Cut "Step 3: Choose the right type of support" down to a short summary and link across.

**Neither draft links to the other.** Both were written before either existed, so this is expected, but it must be fixed at publication: two sibling pages with a third of their substance in common and no link between them is the worst version of this.

## 4. Slugs

Neither document specifies a URL, which is correct given the scope split, but both matter here.

**Outsource admin.** The slug must carry the term, because the term is the whole point of the article. `/guides/how-to-outsource-admin/` matches the how-to intent that editorial content can actually win, and keeps `/outsource-admin/` free for the service page if you build it. Avoid anything containing "administrator" or "jobs".

**Decision guide.** With zero search volume, the slug should optimise for entity clarity rather than keywords, because its readers arrive from AI answers, not from Google. `/guides/ea-vs-va-vs-chief-of-staff/` states the comparison plainly. Avoid a question-shaped slug.
